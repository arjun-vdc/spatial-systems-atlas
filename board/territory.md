## Legend

- `●` built in
- `⊕` through an extension
- `◐` partial
- `○` absent
- `✕` conflicts, will not interoperate
- `–` out of scope
- `(blank)` no claim recorded for that pair

A **format** capability is fixed when the content is made. A **protocol** capability is agreed between two systems when they connect.

The **Boundary** column says how much two independent systems have to agree on that row: `must` means a use case fails outright without agreement, `should` means it finishes with visible loss, and `none` means nothing crosses the boundary at all. The word on each row, and the one sentence that decided it, are in `territory.csv` beside this file.

The notes behind each claim, and the sources each claim rests on, are in `territory.csv` beside this file.

- **Rasterization pipeline** (format) - Turning scene geometry into pixels, including the render graph and pass structure.
- **Graphics interface binding** (format) - Which low-level graphics interface the renderer targets.
- **Material model** (format) - How surface appearance is described and evaluated at render time.
- **Lighting and global illumination** (format) - Direct and indirect light, shadows, probes, baked or real time.
- **Post-processing** (format) - Effects applied after the main render pass.
- **Level of detail and streaming order** (protocol) - Choosing what detail to show and what to send first. An interoperability question once geometry is streamed.
- **Gaussian splatting and radiance fields** (format) - Rendering captured scenes represented as splats or radiance fields rather than meshes.
- **Volumetric and point cloud** (format) - Rendering point clouds, voxels and volumetric video.
- **Text rendering** (format) - Legible text in three dimensions, including fonts and layout.
- **Camera and viewpoint model** (format) - How viewpoints are defined, bound and moved between.
- **Passthrough and environment blending** (format) - Mixing the live camera view of the real world with rendered content, and controlling how much of each the viewer sees, including cutting a hole in a virtual environment to let a real object through.
- **Colour space and dynamic range** (format) - Declaring the color interpretation and output range of images, textures, and rendering surfaces so receivers can perform the required conversions.
- **Two-dimensional interface surfaces** (format) - Layout and presentation of two-dimensional interface regions, including their size, alignment, and relationship to a display or scene.
- **Compositor layers** (protocol) - Submitting distinct shaped image layers to a display compositor with their placement and composition properties.
- **General-purpose GPU computation** (format) - Dispatching programmable non-raster GPU workloads against resources available through the graphics-compute API.
- **Frame pacing and predicted display time** (protocol) - Coordinating frame submission with predicted display timing and the view poses associated with that time.
- **Portable shader language** (format) - A specified shader source language and execution semantics enabling implementations to consume the same shader program.
- **Remote (split) rendering** (protocol) - Rendering a view on a server from the client's predicted pose and streaming the frames back for display, so a device shows content it could not draw itself.

- **World generation from a prompt or an image** (format) - Producing a whole three-dimensional place from a description or a picture, rather than reconstructing one that exists — and whether what comes out is a scene a receiver can keep, or frames it can only watch.
- **Simulation from a learned model** (protocol) - A model that continues a world in response to what the viewer does, producing each next moment itself instead of handing over a scene for an engine to run — with the stated limits on trusting that prediction.
- **Photogrammetric object capture** (format) - Building a textured three-dimensional model from a set of ordinary photographs of a real object.
- **Splat data interchange format** (format) - Carrying Gaussian splat data between tools and runtimes as a named format, as distinct from being able to render splats already in memory.
- **Captured-scene streaming** (protocol) - Delivering a captured scene progressively, by region and by detail level, so a viewer starts before the whole scene has arrived and never holds more than it can draw.
- **Volumetric video capture and playback** (format) - A captured, time-varying three-dimensional scene carried and replayed as media, rather than authored as geometry, including the camera calibration and mask metadata a second implementation would need to reproduce it.
- **Point cloud encoding** (format) - How a set of points, each with a position and zero or more attributes, is encoded and compressed.
- **Multi-view plus depth** (format) - Carrying a scene as several calibrated camera views with per-pixel depth, together with the camera intrinsics and extrinsics needed to reproject them.
- **Novel view synthesis** (format) - Producing an image from a viewing position that no capture camera occupied.
- **Time-varying capture** (protocol) - Carrying a captured scene that changes over time, so that a receiver can play it back at the right rate without being handed each frame as an unrelated file.
- **Relighting captured content** (format) - Making a captured scene respond to the lighting of the place it is dropped into, rather than staying lit as it was on the day it was photographed.
- **Editing a capture after the fact** (format) - Changing a captured scene — cropping, deleting, recolouring — in a way that survives export, and that applies consistently to every frame of a moving capture.
- **Generative editing of a world** (format) - Changing a world that has already been made by describing the change in words or pointing at a region, and growing it past its original edges, with earlier versions kept rather than overwritten.
- **Control over what gets generated** (protocol) - What a generating system accepts as a specification of the world it should make — a description, one or several reference images with their placement, a panorama, a video, a seed, or uploaded coarse geometry — and in which interchange formats that specification may arrive.
- **Deriving conventional assets from a generated scene** (format) - Turning a splat or radiance-field world into the representations a conventional engine already understands — a collision mesh, a mesh for drawing, a panorama, a video — and stating what is lost in each.
- **Action and control exchange** (protocol) - Carrying an agent's actions — poses, joint or gripper values, camera motion — into and out of a system as time-sampled data, with the embodiment, the frame, the units and any normalisation stated, so a second implementation can read them.
- **Panoramic scene imagery** (format) - Carrying a full 360-degree view of a place as an image, with its projection stated, so a receiver can use it as a scene, as a backdrop, or as a light source.
- **Radiance-field reconstruction** (format) - Reconstructing a radiance field or Gaussian representation from observed images and camera information, including optimization controls and a trained scene usable for subsequent view synthesis.
- **Generation of individual 3D objects** (format) - Producing an individual three-dimensional object from a text or image prompt, with geometry or another renderable representation available for placement in a separately authored scene.
